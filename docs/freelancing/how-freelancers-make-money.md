# Chapter 02: How Freelancers Make Money

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand different pricing models and when to use each
- Set appropriate rates based on your skills and market
- Create professional contracts and invoices
- Choose payment methods that work for your situation
- Manage your finances as a freelancer

## Introduction

Making money as a freelancer is about more than just doing good work. You need to understand pricing, contracts, invoicing, and financial management. This chapter covers the practical financial side of freelancing that many new freelancers overlook.

## Why This Matters

Understanding how money works in freelancing helps you:

- Price your services appropriately
- Get paid reliably and on time
- Manage your finances professionally
- Build a sustainable income over time
- Avoid common financial mistakes

## Core Concepts

### Pricing Models

Freelancers use several pricing models:

#### Hourly Rate

You charge per hour of work.

**When to use:**
- Ongoing projects with unclear scope
- Maintenance and support work
- When clients prefer transparency

**Example**: $50/hour × 40 hours = $2,000

**Pros:**
- Simple to understand
- Easy to track
- Fair for unclear scopes

**Cons:**
- Punishes efficiency (faster work = less income)
- Income varies with hours worked
- Clients may watch the clock

#### Project-Based Rate

You charge a fixed price for the entire project.

**When to use:**
- Well-defined projects with clear scope
- When you have experience estimating similar work
- When you want to reward efficiency

**Example**: $5,000 for a complete website

**Pros:**
- Rewards efficiency
- Client knows total cost upfront
- Easier to plan income

**Cons:**
- Risk of scope creep (unpaid extra work)
- Requires accurate estimation
- Can lose money if project takes longer than expected

#### Value-Based Pricing

You charge based on the value your work provides to the client.

**When to use:**
- When your work directly impacts client revenue
- When you have proven results
- When working with established businesses

**Example**: $10,000 for an e-commerce site that will generate $100,000/year

**Pros:**
- Higher potential income
- Rewards expertise and results
- Aligns your success with client success

**Cons:**
- Requires proven track record
- Hard to justify without results
- Not suitable for beginners

#### Retainer

Clients pay a fixed monthly amount for ongoing access to your services.

**When to use:**
- Long-term client relationships
- Regular, predictable work
- When clients need ongoing support

**Example**: $2,000/month for 20 hours of work

**Pros:**
- Predictable income
- Strong client relationships
- Less time spent finding new clients

**Cons:**
- May limit other opportunities
- Requires consistent availability
- Can feel limiting creatively

### Setting Your Rates

#### Factors to Consider

1. **Your experience level**: More experience = higher rates
2. **Your specialized skills**: Rare skills command higher rates
3. **Market rates**: Research what others charge
4. **Cost of living**: Your location affects your expenses
5. **Business costs**: Software, hardware, insurance, taxes
6. **Desired income**: What you need to earn annually

#### Rate Calculation Formula

```
Desired Annual Income + Business Expenses + Taxes + Profit Margin
÷ Billable Hours Per Year
= Hourly Rate
```

**Example:**
- Desired income: $60,000
- Business expenses: $5,000
- Taxes: $15,000
- Profit margin: $10,000
- Total needed: $90,000
- Billable hours: 1,500 (30 hours/week × 50 weeks)
- Hourly rate: $60/hour

**Important**: Not all hours are billable. Administrative work, marketing, and learning are not billable.

### Contracts

A contract protects both you and your client. Essential elements:

1. **Scope of work**: What you will and will not do
2. **Timeline**: When work starts and ends
3. **Payment terms**: How much, when, and how
4. **Revision policy**: How many revisions are included
5. **Intellectual property**: Who owns the work
6. **Termination clause**: How either party can end the agreement
7. **Confidentiality**: Protection of sensitive information

#### Simple Contract Example

```
PROJECT: Website Development
CLIENT: [Client Name]
FREELANCER: [Your Name]

SCOPE:
- Design and develop a 5-page responsive website
- Include contact form
- Mobile-friendly design
- 2 rounds of revisions

TIMELINE:
- Start: [Date]
- First draft: [Date]
- Final delivery: [Date]

PAYMENT:
- Total: $3,000
- 50% upfront: $1,500
- 50% on completion: $1,500
- Payment method: Bank transfer
- Due: Within 7 days of invoice

REVISIONS:
- 2 rounds included
- Additional revisions: $100/hour

INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY:
- Full ownership transfers upon final payment
```

### Invoicing

An invoice is a bill you send to clients. It should include:

1. Your name and contact information
2. Client's name and contact information
3. Invoice number and date
4. Description of work performed
5. Amount due
6. Payment terms and method
7. Due date

**Tips for invoicing:**
- Send invoices promptly
- Follow up if payment is late
- Keep records for tax purposes
- Use professional invoicing tools

### Payment Methods

Common payment methods for freelancers:

| Method | Pros | Cons | Best For |
|--------|------|------|----------|
| Bank transfer | Low fees, reliable | Slow, international fees | Local clients |
| PayPal | Easy, widely used | Higher fees, limited | International clients |
| Stripe | Professional, integrated | Setup required | Online payments |
| Wise (TransferWise) | Low international fees | Setup required | International transfers |
| Cryptocurrency | Low fees, fast | Volatile, limited acceptance | Tech-savvy clients |

### Financial Management

As a freelancer, you must manage your own finances:

1. **Separate accounts**: Keep business and personal finances separate
2. **Emergency fund**: Save 3-6 months of expenses
3. **Tax savings**: Set aside 25-30% of income for taxes
4. **Retirement savings**: Plan for your own retirement
5. **Expense tracking**: Record all business expenses
6. **Insurance**: Consider health, liability, and equipment insurance

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Setting Rates for a Web Developer

Maria is a web developer with 3 years of experience:

- Desired income: $50,000
- Business costs: $3,000
- Taxes: $12,000
- Total needed: $65,000
- Billable hours: 1,200
- Hourly rate: $54/hour

She rounds to $55/hour for hourly work and charges $2,500-$5,000 for website projects.

### Example 2: Contract Negotiation

Omar receives a project request:

**Client**: "Can you build an e-commerce site for $1,000?"

**Omar's response**: "I appreciate the opportunity. Based on the requirements you described, a custom e-commerce site typically ranges from $3,000-$5,000. This includes product catalog, shopping cart, payment integration, and mobile responsiveness. Would you like me to create a detailed proposal?"

**Result**: Omar educates the client and either gets a fair price or politely declines.

### Example 3: Invoice Management

Sarah sends invoices on the 1st and 15th of each month:

- She uses a template with her branding
- She includes detailed descriptions of work
- She sends reminders 3 days before due date
- She follows up on late payments after 7 days

## Freelancer Perspective

Money management is a skill you must develop:

**Key principles:**
- Never work without a contract
- Always get payment upfront (at least partial)
- Track every expense for tax purposes
- Raise your rates as your skills improve
- Diversify your client base to reduce risk
- Save for taxes from every payment

**Common financial mistakes:**
- Not saving for taxes
- Spending business income on personal expenses
- Not having an emergency fund
- Underpricing to win clients
- Not tracking expenses

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Working Without a Contract

Always have a written agreement, even for small projects. A contract protects both parties and prevents misunderstandings.

### Mistake 2: Not Getting Payment Upfront

At minimum, get 25-50% payment upfront. This shows client commitment and covers your initial work.

### Mistake 3: Underpricing

Low prices attract difficult clients and devalue your work. Price based on value, not just competition.

### Mistake 4: Not Tracking Expenses

Every business expense reduces your taxable income. Track everything: software, hardware, internet, office space, travel.

### Mistake 5: Ignoring Taxes

Set aside money for taxes from every payment. Consider quarterly estimated tax payments to avoid penalties.
